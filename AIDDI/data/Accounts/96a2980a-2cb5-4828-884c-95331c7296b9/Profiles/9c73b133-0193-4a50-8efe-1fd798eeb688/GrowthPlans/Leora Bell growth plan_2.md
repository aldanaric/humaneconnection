# 30-day Growth Plan

**Employee:** Leora Bell

**Role:** Math teacher

**Type:** INTJ-A (Architect)

**Core Functions:** Develop and deliver engaging lessons, facilitate classroom discussions, assess student learning, maintain a positive learning environment, collaborate with colleagues, and manage administrative tasks.

## 1. Purpose and Framework
This plan will focus on enhancing Leora's classroom communication clarity, fostering empathetic student engagement, and refining her approach to delivering actionable feedback, all to improve student understanding and learning outcomes.

It makes use of the three concepts of:
-   **Humane Connection&trade;**:Presence, Empathy, Clarity, Adaptability, Recognition of Humanity
-   **Owning Your Work**:Define, Choose, Zoom Out, Submit, Absorb, Teach.
-   **Behavior-Change science**:
    *   **Habit Loop (Charles Duhigg):** Explains how habits are formed through a cue, routine, and reward, providing a framework for altering automatic behaviors like speaking pace.
    *   **Fogg Behavior Model (B.J. Fogg):** Posits that behavior (B) happens when motivation (M), ability (A), and a prompt (P) converge, guiding us to make desired actions easier to perform and well-cued.

## 2. Development Priorities

**Development Priority 1: Enhance Communication Clarity and Pacing**

**Behavioral goal:**Always maintain a deliberate and appropriate speaking pace during lessons, ensuring all students can follow complex mathematical concepts.

**Humane Connection Link:** Directly leverages **Clarity** by ensuring information is delivered at an understandable rate and **Presence** by being attuned to student comprehension.

**Method:** Leora will integrate deliberate pauses and regular checks for understanding into her lesson delivery. This targets the "speaking too quickly" observation. By leveraging the **Fogg Behavior Model**, we aim to increase her *ability* to pace herself through structured techniques and *prompts* (visual cues, mental reminders). For her INTJ nature, the logical efficiency of clear, measured communication for better student outcomes will serve as motivation.

**Behavior-Change Design:**
-   **Cue:** Transitioning between major lesson points; observing student facial expressions (e.g., puzzled look).
-   **Routine:** Pause for 3-5 seconds; ask a specific question ("What's one key step here?"); count silently to three before continuing.
-   **Reward:** Noticing increased student participation; students correctly answering questions; seeing clearer understanding on exit tickets.

**Supervisor Role:** Observe Leora's lessons, providing specific, data-driven feedback on her speaking pace and the impact on student engagement.

**Development Priority 2: Foster Empathetic Engagement and Responsiveness**

**Behavioral goal:** Actively solicit and respond to both verbal and non-verbal student cues indicating confusion, understanding, or a need for clarification.

**Humane Connection Link:** Emphasizes **Empathy** by understanding student learning challenges and **Adaptability** by adjusting instruction based on real-time feedback. This supports her growth in "developing emotional intelligence" and strengthening relationships (Analyst Personalities, page 2).

**Method:** Leora will implement structured check-in points (e.g., "thumbs up/down/sideways," quick poll questions) and practice active observation of student body language during instruction. This uses the **Fogg Behavior Model** by providing clear *prompts* for student feedback and increasing Leora's *ability* to interpret and respond to it, turning passive observation into an active routine.

**Supervisor Role:** Help Leora identify specific non-verbal cues to look for, discuss strategies for pausing and adjusting, and debrief observed student interactions.

**Development Priority 3: Deliver Constructive and Actionable Feedback**

**Behavioral goal:** Provide feedback on student work that is precise, solution-oriented, and communicated in a supportive manner that motivates improvement.

**Humane Connection Link:** Upholds **Recognition of Humanity** by valuing student effort and **Clarity** by offering a clear path for growth, addressing her "overly blunt feedback" tendency.

**Method:** Leora will practice framing feedback using a "feedforward" approach, focusing on specific actions students can take to improve, rather than just identifying errors. This method also involves explicitly linking feedback to success criteria. This links to the **Fogg Behavior Model** by increasing the *ability* of students to act on feedback, thereby increasing Leora's *motivation* to refine her delivery given the positive impact.

**Supervisor Role:** Review examples of Leora's written and verbal feedback, offering coaching on how to balance directness with supportive framing.

## 4. Supervisory Environment for Growth

| Practice | Why It Matters | Application |

## 5. Success Indicators
*   **Communication Clarity & Pace:** Students consistently demonstrate understanding of concepts immediately after explanation, with fewer requests for repetition or clarification.
*   **Empathetic Engagement:** Leora proactively adjusts her instruction or re-explains concepts based on observed student non-verbal cues and direct check-ins.
*   **Constructive & Actionable Feedback:** Student assignments show evidence of students incorporating feedback into subsequent work, demonstrating improved reasoning or accuracy.

## 6. Sustaining High Performance
1.  **Identity Anchor**: I am a precise and empathetic communicator who empowers every student to grasp complex ideas.
2.  **Environmental Cue:** Before I begin a new topic, I will take a deep breath and scan the room.
3.  **Employee specific check in habit to reinforce method effectiveness:** At the end of each lesson, Leora will briefly reflect on her pacing and two moments she actively responded to student cues, noting what worked well.
4.  **30-Day Checkpoint:** Review student work and engagement data, gather informal student feedback, and discuss Leora's self-reflection to determine next steps for deeper integration or new growth areas.

## Conclusion
This plan will help Leora refine her natural INTJ strengths in logic and efficiency by intentionally integrating empathetic communication and responsiveness, directly addressing the impact of her rapid speech. By systematically practicing new behaviors and leveraging targeted supervisory support, Leora will enhance student comprehension and build stronger classroom connections.

### How Performance Thrives Through Humane Connection&trade;
-   **Presence builds psychological safety** When managers listen actively and respond thoughtfully, employees feel seen and heard. This reduces fear-based shutdowns and encourages real-time inquiry, which improves task clarity and execution.
-   **Empathy fosters resilience and engagement** Stepping into an employee’s perspective helps managers tailor support and recognize effort, not just outcomes. This strengthens trust and motivates sustained effort, especially during complex or ambiguous tasks.
-   **Recognition of Humanity deepens accountability** When people feel their worth is acknowledged, they’re more likely to take ownership of their work. This expression reinforces dignity, which drives intrinsic motivation and pride in contribution.
-   **Clarity reduces rework and accelerates progress** Clear, candid, and kind communication ensures expectations are understood. It minimizes confusion, enables better planning, and allows employees to self-correct before errors compound.
-   **Adaptability increases effectiveness across differences** Adjusting tone and delivery to meet individual contexts helps managers connect across styles and stress responses. This flexibility improves coaching outcomes and team cohesion.

### Why Behavior-Change Science Amplifies These Effects
-   **It transforms insight into habit** Humane Connection creates the conditions for trust and reflection. Behavior-change science ensures those insights are translated into repeatable actions through cues, reinforcement, and feedback loops.
-   **It targets the root, not just the symptom** Instead of reacting to performance gaps, behavior-change frameworks help managers identify triggers, beliefs, and environmental factors that shape behavior, leading to more sustainable growth.
-   **It builds adaptive capacity** By combining emotional resonance with behavioral modeling, managers help employees navigate uncertainty, build new skills, and internalize ownership.
