# 30-day Growth Plan

**Employee:** Grace-Oriana Andre

**Role:** Impact Investment Analyst

**Type:** INTJ-A (Architect)

**Core Functions:** Grace-Oriana's role involves comprehensive research and analysis of portfolio investments, financial modeling and performance monitoring tied to mission impact, and creating documentation and communications for internal and external stakeholders. She also contributes to team collaboration, builds external relationships, and supports the Director of Investments in managing the endowment and mission alignment metrics.

## 1. Purpose and Framework
This plan focuses on empowering Grace-Oriana to proactively seek clarification, navigate ambiguity with greater confidence, and enhance her comprehension of complex analytical tasks.

It makes use of the three concepts of:
-   **Humane Connection&trade;**:Presence, Empathy, Clarity, Adaptability, Recognition of Humanity
-   **Owning Your Work**:Define, Choose, Zoom Out, Submit, Absorb, Teach.
-   **Behavior-Change science**:
    *   **BJ Fogg's Tiny Habits**: For creating small, easily repeatable actions to build new behaviors.
    *   **Carol Dweck's Growth Mindset**: For fostering a belief that abilities can be developed through dedication and hard work, reframing challenges as learning opportunities.

## 2. Development Priorities

**Development Priority 1: Proactive Clarification and Inquiry**

**Behavioral goal:** Grace-Oriana will proactively ask clarifying questions on tasks at the point of assignment or initial engagement, ensuring full understanding before proceeding.

**Humane Connection Link:** This links to **Clarity** by fostering open communication to ensure shared understanding and **Presence** by encouraging active engagement with tasks and supervisors from the outset.

**Method:** Implement a "3-Question Rule" using BJ Fogg's Tiny Habits. After receiving a task, Grace-Oriana will immediately formulate and ask at least three clarifying questions (even if she feels she understands, she will push for deeper inquiry or edge cases). The supervisor will reinforce this positively regardless of the question's perceived simplicity.

**Behavior-Change Design:**
-   **Cue:** Task assigned or email received for new task.
-   **Routine:** Immediately (within 5 minutes) write down/formulate 3 clarifying questions. Ask them in person or via message.
-   **Reward:** Supervisor's explicit positive affirmation for asking questions ("Thank you for these thoughtful questions, Grace-Oriana!"). Feeling of clarity and reduced potential rework.

**Supervisor Role:** Consistently prompt for questions, provide specific positive reinforcement for *any* question asked, and create a safe space by normalizing inquiry ("No question is too small"). Offer examples of types of questions she might ask.

---

**Development Priority 2: Navigating Uncertainty with Support**

**Behavioral goal:** Grace-Oriana will actively identify moments of uncertainty during tasks and promptly communicate her need for guidance or support to her supervisor.

**Humane Connection Link:** This links to **Empathy** by allowing the supervisor to understand her perspective and provide tailored support, and **Adaptability** by enabling real-time adjustments to ensure progress.

**Method:** Apply Carol Dweck's Growth Mindset principles by reframing uncertainty as a critical point for learning and engagement, rather than a failure. Grace-Oriana will practice a "Pause-Identify-Share" method. When she encounters a "freezing" moment, she will pause, identify the specific point of uncertainty, and immediately draft a communication (email/message) to her supervisor articulating the challenge and a potential next step or question.

**Behavior-Change Design:**
-   **Cue:** Encountering a mental "freeze" or feeling stuck on a task.
-   **Routine:** Pause, identify the specific point of confusion, and draft a message to the supervisor stating the problem and requesting guidance.
-   **Reward:** The supervisor responds promptly with supportive, non-judgmental guidance. Sense of progress unblocked, reduced internal stress.

**Supervisor Role:** Regularly check in on progress (not just outcomes), explicitly praise the act of seeking help early, and frame challenges as opportunities for skill development. Reassure Grace-Oriana that asking for help is a strength.

---

**Development Priority 3: Deepening Analytical Task Comprehension**

**Behavioral goal:** Grace-Oriana will demonstrate a deeper understanding of analytical frameworks and methodologies through proactive self-correction and accurate application.

**Humane Connection Link:** This links to **Recognition of Humanity** by validating her intellectual drive for mastery, and **Clarity** by building robust internal models of complex concepts.

**Method:** Utilize the "Teach-Back" approach within the "Owning Your Work" framework, combined with BJ Fogg's concept of habit stacking. After engaging with a new analytical concept or completing an analytical task, Grace-Oriana will create a brief, internal "teach-back" summary (e.g., a short bulleted email or 5-minute verbal explanation) to her supervisor or a designated peer. This ritual will follow the completion of *any* analytical module or significant task.

**Behavior-Change Design:**
-   **Cue:** Completion of an analytical module/training or submission of an analytical task.
-   **Routine:** Create a brief "teach-back" summary explaining the concept/process and its application, then share it with the supervisor/peer.
-   **Reward:** Supervisor/peer provides confirming or constructive feedback, solidifying understanding. Feeling of mastery and intellectual satisfaction, which energizes an INTJ.

**Supervisor Role:** Be available for "teach-back" sessions, provide affirming and specific feedback on her explanations, and offer additional resources or deeper dives where appropriate.

## Conclusion
This growth plan is designed to help Grace-Oriana Andre leverage her innate analytical strengths by developing crucial interpersonal and self-advocacy skills. By fostering proactive inquiry and a growth mindset around uncertainty, she will deepen her comprehension of complex analytical tasks, enhance team collaboration, and ultimately drive greater impact in her role.

### How Performance Thrives Through Humane Connection&trade;
-   **Presence builds psychological safety** When managers listen actively and
    respond thoughtfully, employees feel seen and heard. This reduces fear-
    based shutdowns and encourages real-time inquiry, which improves task
    clarity and execution.
-   **Empathy fosters resilience and engagement** Stepping into an employee’s
    perspective helps managers tailor support and recognize effort, not just
    outcomes. This strengthens trust and motivates sustained effort, especially
    during complex or ambiguous tasks.
-   **Recognition of Humanity deepens accountability** When people feel their
    worth is acknowledged, they’re more likely to take ownership of their work.
    This expression reinforces dignity, which drives intrinsic motivation and pride
    in contribution.
-   **Clarity reduces rework and accelerates progress** Clear, candid, and kind
    communication ensures expectations are understood. It minimizes
    confusion, enables better planning, and allows employees to self-correct
    before errors compound.
-   **Adaptability increases effectiveness across differences** Adjusting tone
    and delivery to meet individual contexts helps managers connect across
    styles and stress responses. This flexibility improves coaching outcomes and
    team cohesion.

### Why Behavior-Change Science Amplifies These Effects
- **It transforms insight into habit** Humane Connection creates the conditions
  for trust and reflection. Behavior-change science ensures those insights are
  translated into repeatable actions through cues, reinforcement, and
  feedback loops.
- **It targets the root, not just the symptom** Instead of reacting to
  performance gaps, behavior-change frameworks help managers identify
  triggers, beliefs, and environmental factors that shape behavior, leading to
  more sustainable growth.
- **It builds adaptive capacity** By combining emotional resonance with
  behavioral modeling, managers help employees navigate uncertainty, build
  new skills, and internalize ownership.
