# Team Diagnostics Output Format

Generate only the sections requested by the user. Use the exact headings below.

Match the tone and structure of a facilitator-ready training packet: practical, warm, specific, no emojis.

---

## Team Dynamics Blueprint

A team-level section titled **Evaluating [Team Name] as a Team**. Include:

### Personality Distribution
Temperament groups (Analysts, Diplomats, Sentinels, Explorers) with each member named, typed, and briefly described. Include trait percentages from the assessments when available (e.g., 83% Extraverted, 81% Intuitive).

### Balance of Introverts vs. Extraverts
Who leans where, with percentages when available, and what that means for team energy.

### Identity Traits: Assertive vs. Turbulent
How assertive/turbulent balance affects stress, decision-making, and resilience. Name each member and include percentages when available.

### Communication Styles
Per-member communication tendencies and team-level patterns.

### Strength Clusters
Group strengths under headings such as Leadership & Influence, Innovation, Support & Collaboration.

### Conflict Styles
How each member tends to handle conflict.

### Potential Growth Edges
Collective friction areas: stress, criticism, delegation, idealism vs. practicality.

### For the ED to Consider
A leadership-focused subsection on macro team patterns. Cover themes such as:
- Predominance of Intuitive (N) types and what that means for big-picture thinking vs. practical detail
- Incidence of Turbulent vs. Assertive identity across the team
- Extravert/introvert balance and what the quieter voices may need
- Feeling vs. Thinking balance and risk of emotional overload or harmony over hard truths

## At Your Best / Under Stress — [Full Name]

Open with 2–3 sentences on how this person shows up when thriving at work. Include their full type (e.g., INTJ-T Turbulent Architect). Write entirely in **second person** ("you").

Include a paragraph on their **ideal working environment** — what conditions help them perform best.

Include this disclaimer:
"Please remember that results from the 16 Personalities test may not fully apply to you, as many other factors such as background, context, and personal experiences also play a significant role."

7 bullets, each starting with "You…"

7 bullets, each starting with "You…"

## Coaching Cards — [Full Name]

**Type:** [e.g., ENFJ-T Turbulent Protagonist]

For facilitator breakout conversations. Extract **5 recurring patterns** from this person's assessment.

For each pattern:

### Pattern [N]: [Short name]

**Talking point:**
Begin with acknowledgment of a strength, then use the phrase **"The pattern I see is…"** to name the shadow side supportively. Write as the facilitator speaking to this person.

**Reflection question:**
One open coaching question. Not an evaluation.

---

## Pair Discussion Guide — [Person A] × [Person B]

Use a **Working Together** structure. Include a brief intro sentence on what this pairing brings together.

### Complementary Strengths
3–5 bullet points on how their styles can work together productively.

### Potential Friction & Best Interaction Strategies
Cover relevant dimensions such as communication, decision-making, conflict, and delegation where applicable. Be specific to these two people's types — not generic advice.

### Discussion Point
One open question for a 10–15 minute pair conversation. **Address both people by first name** and name their specific style difference directly.

Example format:
"[Name A], you tend to… while [Name B], you prefer…. How can you…?"

Do not use generic prompts like "reflect on a recent project."

---

## Assembly rules

- Generate only requested sections.
- For individualized sections, repeat per team member.
- For pair guides, include every unique pair.
- Frame all language for the specified audience.
- Pull trait percentages and type details from the assessment inputs — do not invent them.
